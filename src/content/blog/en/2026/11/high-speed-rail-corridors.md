Track capacity on a railway corridor is governed less by maximum train speed than by **speed homogeneity**. When 250 km/h passenger expresses share rails with 100 km/h intermodal freight trains, overtake loops and braking buffer zones consume the majority of theoretical timetable slots.

## Speed Differentials and Timetable Consumption

On a conventional double-track line, inserting a single high-speed train into a steady stream of regional and freight services requires clearing a long headway corridor ahead of the fast train.

### Moving-Block Signaling Gains

Upgrading legacy fixed-block color-light signals to continuous cab signaling (such as ETCS Level 2 or digital ATC) reduces safe headway intervals by 20–35%, allowing denser freight platoons once fastest passenger services move to dedicated tracks.

## Traction Power and 25 kV AC Substations

Sustaining short headways on heavy freight and high-speed corridors requires 25 kV 50/60 Hz autotransformer feeding systems capable of absorbing regenerative braking energy without triggering over-voltage trips at adjacent substations.
